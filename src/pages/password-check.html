Checks the password against hundreds of millions of leaked passwords collected by Have I Been Pwned. The password itself never leaves your browser.

What gets sent

Your browser turns the password into a SHA-1 hash, then sends only the first 5 characters. Have I Been Pwned replies with every leaked hash starting with those 5 characters, hundreds of them, and your browser looks for a match privately. This is called k-anonymity.

What happens to this password

  • Hashed on your device. The password is never sent anywhere, to us or to Have I Been Pwned.
  • Only 5 characters leave. The first 5 characters of the hash match hundreds of other leaked passwords, so they reveal nothing about yours.
  • Not logged. We have no server that could log it. This site is static files.

Reading the result

  • Found: stop using it everywhere. Attackers try leaked passwords first.
  • Not found: good, but it doesn't prove the password is strong. "Summer2026!" may not be listed yet and would still fall quickly.

About Have I Been Pwned

Pwned Passwords is a free database of passwords exposed in real data breaches, run by security researcher Troy Hunt. Password managers and identity services use it to warn people about leaked passwords. NIST's digital identity guidelines tell services to check new passwords against exactly this kind of list.
